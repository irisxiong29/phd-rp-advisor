# Workflow Stages

Use this file after identifying the student's current stage.

## Stage 1 — Icebreaker preparation

### Objective
Prepare a slide-ready diagnostic lesson, not a final research direction.

### Evidence to extract
From CV:
- disciplinary training
- dissertation/project topics
- relevant employment/practice
- methodological exposure
- language/field access
- plausible research continuity

From supervisor-choice table:
- target disciplines/programmes
- supervisor themes
- repeated interests across choices
- tensions between the student's background and target field

When supervisor fit depends on current research and web access is available:
- verify the supervisor's current institutional profile
- check recent publications/projects rather than relying only on an old table
- separate verified recent interests from speculative fit

From writing sample:
- actual theories used
- whether concepts are defined or only named
- methods used
- analytical strengths
- recurring weaknesses
- academic English level

### Default deliverable
A 6–8 slide content structure. If a PPT is explicitly requested and presentation tooling is
available, create the actual `.pptx`; otherwise return slide-ready content.


1. Student research profile
2. Research-interest map
3. Existing theoretical familiarity
4. Existing methodological familiarity
5. Potential research areas (broad, provisional)
6. Supervisor/programme fit
7. Questions to ask during the icebreaker
8. What evidence is still needed / next step

### Question design
Ask questions that reveal:
- what the student genuinely wants to spend 3–4 years studying
- which cases/communities/materials they can access
- whether they prefer textual, audience, industry, policy, design, platform, or ethnographic research
- what they found most convincing/frustrating in prior research
- what they do not want to study
- future academic/professional direction
- constraints on geography, language, fieldwork, or sensitive populations

Do not make the lesson sound as if the topic is already decided.

## Stage 2 — Topic brainstorming

### Objective
Produce 3–4 distinct, expandable directions after the icebreaker.

### Required fields for each topic
1. Working title
2. Why this direction fits the student's evidence
3. Core phenomenon and research problem
4. Core research question
5. Expansion logic / possible sub-questions
6. Possible theoretical architecture
   - core theory/concepts
   - supporting concepts if needed
7. Methods and likely data
8. Core benchmark literature
9. Advantages
10. Risks / unresolved questions
11. Adaptability across supervisor/programme contexts

### Distinctness test
Do not offer four titles that all ask the same question about different cases.
The options should vary meaningfully in at least one of:
- research problem
- analytical level
- theoretical conversation
- data source
- disciplinary positioning

### Literature target
For each option, prefer 3–6 high-quality benchmark items:
- close empirical precedents
- key theoretical works
- recent field-defining work where relevant

More references are not automatically better.

## Stage 3 — 800-word outline

### Objective
Build a compact argument map that the student can expand.

### Target length
Default to approximately 750–900 English words excluding references and Chinese annotations.
If the user specifies another limit, follow it.

### Word allocation heuristic
- Titles: not counted
- Abstract: 110–150
- Introduction: 140–190
- Literature Review/Theoretical Frames: 250–320
- Research Gap + RQ logic: 80–120
- Methodology: 120–170
- Expected Contribution: 40–70

Do not force exact counts if that harms coherence.

### Supervisor adaptation
Provide 2–4 alternative titles only when they make genuine sense.
Alternatives may shift emphasis toward:
- media/communication
- cultural studies
- sociology/anthropology
- design/heritage
- business/consumer research
- another target discipline

Do not produce cosmetic synonym swaps.

## Stage 4 — Chinese annotated outline

For each section provide:
- 此部分的功能
- 学生需要扩写什么
- 推荐段落顺序
- 关键概念/文献应放在哪里
- 与前后文的逻辑关系
- 常见错误
- 建议字数（如有整体字数要求）

For RQs explain:
- 主问题如何从 gap 推导
- 次问题分别承担什么分析任务
- 不必替学生一次写死所有次问题，除非用户要求

For Literature Review:
- explain what argument each subsection needs to establish
- distinguish "文献罗列" from "文献对话"
- identify where the student's own positioning should emerge

## Stage 5 — Full draft review

### Review order
1. Overall argument
2. Research problem and gap
3. Literature accuracy and coverage
4. Theory–RQ alignment
5. Methodology–RQ alignment
6. Section and paragraph transitions
7. Scope and feasibility
8. Contribution
9. Language/style

Do not begin with grammar unless grammar blocks comprehension.

### Default output
A. Overall assessment
B. Major issues (must fix)
C. Moderate issues (should fix)
D. Minor issues (polish)
E. Section-by-section comments
F. Source-verification notes
G. Recommended revision order

---

## Stage 6 — Final proofreading

### Objective
Preserve the student's revised argument and voice.

Check:
- grammar and articles
- verb tense
- agreement
- prepositions
- collocation
- terminology consistency
- awkward nominalisation
- vague pronouns
- sentence-level logic
- paragraph transition
- duplicated claims
- citation/reference consistency

Prefer minimal edits.
If a sentence is acceptable, do not rewrite it merely to make it sound more sophisticated.
