# Style and Authorship Guide

Use this module for Stage 3, Stage 4 when English text is retained, Stage 5 language comments,
and especially Stage 6 final proofreading.

## Default English

Unless the target programme requires otherwise:
- British spelling
- clear formal academic tone
- moderate sentence length
- direct grammatical subjects
- specialist vocabulary only where it adds precision
- single quotation marks where appropriate

## Preserve student voice

Treat the student's writing sample and draft as evidence of their normal linguistic range.

During editing:
1. Correct clear errors.
2. Improve meaning where wording is ambiguous.
3. Simplify inflated phrasing.
4. Preserve acceptable student wording.
5. Rewrite whole paragraphs only when local editing cannot repair the logic.

Do not flatten the entire document into one highly polished, uniform voice.

## Common model-like patterns to reduce

These are style risks, not forbidden forms.

Watch for excessive:
- three-part parallel lists in consecutive sentences
- repeated "not X but Y" or "not only...but also..." contrasts
- "while X..., Y..." used mechanically at the start of many paragraphs
- abstract nouns piled together
- interchangeable verbs such as "highlight", "underscore", "illuminate", "foreground"
- generic framing such as "In today's rapidly evolving..."
- "complex interplay", "multifaceted", "nuanced", "dynamic landscape" without analytical content
- repetitive topic sentences with the same rhythm
- overly neat concluding sentences after every paragraph
- needless em dashes and semicolons
- dense transitions such as "Moreover/Furthermore/Additionally" in every paragraph

Do not remove a phrase merely because it appears on this list if it is genuinely the clearest wording.

## Vocabulary

Prefer the simplest accurate word.

Examples:
- "use" before "utilise" unless register requires otherwise
- "show" or "demonstrate" before "elucidate"
- "change" before "transformation" when the stronger noun is not analytically necessary

Keep disciplinary terms such as:
- platformisation
- affect
- remediation
- authenticity
- performativity
when they are theoretically necessary and properly defined.

## Sentence rhythm

Natural academic prose contains:
- short analytical sentences
- medium explanatory sentences
- occasional longer sentences for qualification

Do not force every sentence to the same length or syntactic complexity.

## Editing for authorship integrity

A final proofread should not be treated as detector manipulation.

Do:
- identify abrupt changes in vocabulary/register
- flag passages that sound unlike the rest of the student's draft
- return to terminology already used accurately by the student
- remove generic filler
- ask whether a claim reflects the student's actual argument if it appears newly inserted

Do not:
- insert deliberate grammar mistakes
- use random synonym replacement
- degrade clarity to look "human"
- guarantee a Turnitin or other AI-detector outcome

## Outline-specific rule

An outline should sound like a strong student planning a project, not like a published journal article.
Leave room for the student to expand, justify, and refine claims in the full RP.
